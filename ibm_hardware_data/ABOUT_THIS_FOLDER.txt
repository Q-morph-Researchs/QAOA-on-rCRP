ABOUT THIS FOLDER: IBM_HARDWARE_DATA
====================================

Short version: the raw results from the real IBM quantum computer (ibm_fez):
all 40 jobs and 80,000 shots, the compiled circuits that actually ran, and
IBM's calibration data. Nothing here was changed after the jobs came back,
except that our IBM account name and instance ID were blanked out.

WHAT'S IN HERE
- jobs/: one folder per job with its measured results and its job record.
- circuits/: the compiled circuits and IBM calibration snapshots for the 10
  circuit versions that ran.
- frozen_angles.json: the angle records. The ones used on hardware are named
  like loo:random-S3T4C6-21:p2 (predicate) and fresh:galle:p2 (QUBO); see
  paper Appendix Table A1.
- study.json: the study setup (backend, settings, the 40 job slots).
- summary.csv, summary.json: a summary per pairing, straight from the study
  program. "accepted_rate" is the paper's legal-plan rate;
  "replay_optimal_rate" is the optimal-plan rate.
- events.jsonl: a timestamped log of everything the study did, one JSON
  object per line.

HEADS-UP
- In IBM's bit strings, qubit 0 is the rightmost character.
- Every job worked first time: no failures and no resubmissions.
- Jobs 1 to 26 ran on 12 October, job 27 on 14 October and jobs 28 to 40 on
  16 October, with circuits recompiled for each date.
- For legal and optimal counts per job without decoding anything yourself,
  open results/tables/all_job_rates.csv.

NAMES USED IN THE FILES
- random-S3T4C6-21 = bay A, random-S3T4C6-24 = bay B, random-S3T4C7-32 = bay
  C, random-S4T3C6-16 = bay D, data3-3-8 = bay E.
- "galle" = the QUBO-based encoding (named after the Galle et al. model).
  "predicate" = the predicate-based encoding.
- "accepted" = what the paper calls a legal plan. "replay-optimal" = an
  optimal plan.
