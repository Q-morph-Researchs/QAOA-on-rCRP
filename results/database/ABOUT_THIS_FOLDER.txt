ABOUT THIS FOLDER: DATABASE
===========================

All the hardware results in one database, down to every single shot (80,000
rows). Same data as the CSV files here, just in one place.

QUICKEST START
- Headline numbers: analysis_summary.csv.
- The 40 jobs: job_rates.csv.
- To explore: open ibm_results.sqlite in any SQLite viewer (for example the
  free DB Browser for SQLite, https://sqlitebrowser.org). Example queries
  are in code/analysis/queries.sql.

TABLES IN THE DATABASE (ONE ROW IS...)
- instances: one bay (stacks, tier limit, proven optimum, source). This is
  the paper's Table 1.
- pairings: one bay plus encoding (8 rows).
- preparations: one circuit version, including the frozen angles.
- jobs: one IBM job (40 rows).
- shots: one measurement (80,000 rows).
- histograms: one bit string's count within one job.
- outcomes: one observed bit string, decoded: legal or not, optimal or not,
  relocations, energy and moves.
- job_rates and shot_outcomes: ready-made views joining the above.
- summary_metrics: summary statistics per pairing. METHODS_AND_ANALYSIS.md
  explains the method and VALIDATION.json records the checks the database
  passed.

HEADS-UP
- "state" is the integer from IBM's bit string, with qubit 0 as the
  rightmost bit. outcomes.bitstring keeps IBM's display order, so don't
  reverse it again.
- An empty cell (NULL) means "not defined", not zero.
- Energies are encoding-specific, so don't compare a QUBO energy with a
  predicate energy.
- Angle lists give the gamma values first, then the beta values.

NAMES USED IN THE FILES
- random-S3T4C6-21 = bay A, random-S3T4C6-24 = bay B, random-S3T4C7-32 = bay
  C, random-S4T3C6-16 = bay D, data3-3-8 = bay E.
- "galle" = the QUBO-based encoding (named after the Galle et al. model).
  "predicate" = the predicate-based encoding.
- "accepted" = what the paper calls a legal plan. "replay-optimal" = an
  optimal plan.
